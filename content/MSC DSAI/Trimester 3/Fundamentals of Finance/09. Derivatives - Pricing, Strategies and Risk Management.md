## 1. Foundations of Derivatives Markets

In traditional financial markets, an **asset** is an instrument with intrinsic economic value that generates future cash flows. For example, owning shares of a company entitles the shareholder to potential dividends and a claim on residual earnings. The fundamental value of such an asset is determined by discounting its expected future cash flows to the present value.

A **derivative**, by contrast, possesses no independent existence or intrinsic cash-flow generation of its own. It is a legally binding financial contract whose value is entirely derived from the price, volatility, and performance of another asset, known as the **underlying asset**.

```
+--------------------------+         Derives Value From         +----------------------------------+
|     Derivative Contract  |  ===============================>  |         Underlying Asset         |
|  (Call / Put / Futures)  |                                    | (Equity Shares, Indices, Bonds)  |
+--------------------------+                                    +----------------------------------+
```

### The Underlying Asset Principle

A derivative contract cannot exist in isolation. Just as fire insurance cannot be underwritten without a physical property to insure, a financial derivative requires a reference asset. In equity derivatives markets, underlying assets commonly include:

- **Individual Equity Shares:** Standardized shares of publicly listed companies (e.g., Zomato / Eternal Ltd., Reliance, TCS).
    
      
    
- **Market Indices:** Baskets of representative securities tracking broad market movements (e.g., Nifty 50, BSE Sensex).
    
      
    

### Core Economic Functions of Derivatives

Derivatives serve two distinct market functions:

  

1. **Risk Management (Hedging / Insurance):** Protecting an existing portfolio or cash flow against adverse future price movements. An investor locks in a guaranteed price level to establish a safety floor.
    
      
    
2. **Speculation (Leveraged Exposure):** Expressing a directional market view (bullish or bearish) with minimal upfront capital commitment. Derivatives provide asymmetric leverage—magnifying potential percentage returns while defining maximum capital risk for the buyer.
    
      
    

## 2. Anatomy and Standardization of Option Contracts

An **option** is a standardized derivative contract granting its buyer the **right, but not the obligation**, to buy or sell a specific quantity of an underlying asset at a predetermined price on or before a specified expiration date.

  

### Key Specifications of an Option Contract

- **Underlying Asset ($S$):** The specific financial instrument upon which the contract is written.
    
      
    
- **Spot Price ($S_t$):** The prevailing, real-time market price of the underlying asset in the cash market.
    
      
    
- **Strike Price ($K$):** The fixed, pre-agreed contractual transaction price at which the option holder can exercise their right to buy or sell.
    
      
    
- **Expiration Date ($T$):** The final date on which the contract is valid. At market close on this date, unexercised options expire worthless. In organized Indian markets, standardized equity options typically expire on the designated monthly expiry day (e.g., the last Tuesday/Thursday of the month).
    
      
    
- **Lot Size (Contract Multiplier):** The minimum standardized number of underlying shares bundled into a single tradeable contract. Retail traders cannot buy fractional option units; they must transact in whole lots (e.g., 1 lot = 1,000 shares).
    
      
    
- **Option Premium ($P$ or $C$ / $P$):** The market price paid upfront by the option buyer to the option seller (writer) per share. This is the **only variable** in an option contract that fluctuates continuously during market hours based on supply and demand.
    
      
    

### Expiry Cycles

Exchanges maintain continuous market liquidity by offering multiple standardized expiration horizons simultaneously:

  

|**Expiration Category**|**Description**|
|---|---|
|**Near-Month Contract**|Expires in the current calendar month. Represents the highest trading volume and liquidity.|
|**Next-Month Contract**|Expires at the end of the subsequent calendar month.|
|**Far-Month Contract**|Expires at the end of the second subsequent calendar month.|

_When the near-month contract reaches maturity and expires, the next-month contract rolls forward to become the new near-month contract, and a new far-month contract is simultaneously introduced by the exchange._

  

## 3. Call Options (The Right to Buy)

A **Call Option** grants the contract buyer the legal right—without any obligation—to **purchase** the underlying asset at the strike price ($K$) on or before the expiry date ($T$).

  

```
[ Long Call (Buyer) ]  --- Pays Premium Upfront --->  [ Short Call (Seller/Writer) ]
                       <--- Grants Right to Buy ----
```

### Participant Positions

#### 1. Long Call (Call Buyer)

- **Market Outlook:** Bullish (expects underlying price to rise significantly).
    
      
    
- **Cash Flow:** Immediate cash outflow (pays the total premium = $\text{Premium} \times \text{Lot Size}$).
    
      
    
- **Right vs. Obligation:** Holds the **right** to buy at strike price $K$.
    
      
    
- **Maximum Loss:** Strictly capped at the total premium paid.
    
      
    
- **Maximum Gain:** Theoretically unlimited as the asset price rises ($S_T \to \infty$).
    
      
    

#### 2. Short Call (Call Seller / Writer)

- **Market Outlook:** Bearish or Neutral (expects price to stay below or at strike price $K$).
    
      
    
- **Cash Flow:** Immediate cash inflow (receives total premium).
    
      
    
- **Right vs. Obligation:** Holds the **absolute obligation** to deliver/sell shares at $K$ if the buyer exercises.
    
      
    
- **Maximum Gain:** Strictly capped at the upfront premium received.
    
      
    
- **Maximum Loss:** Theoretically unlimited if the underlying price increases indefinitely.
    
      
    

### Payoff Mathematics at Expiration

For a Call Option with strike price $K$ and premium $C$:

  

$$\text{Gross Payoff (Long Call)} = \max(S_T - K, 0)$$

$$\text{Net Profit/Loss (Long Call)} = \max(S_T - K, 0) - C$$

$$\text{Net Profit/Loss (Short Call)} = C - \max(S_T - K, 0)$$

$$\text{Break-Even Point (BEP)} = K + C$$

## 4. Put Options (The Right to Sell)

A **Put Option** grants the contract buyer the legal right—without any obligation—to **sell** the underlying asset at the strike price ($K$) on or before the expiry date ($T$).

  

```
[ Long Put (Buyer) ]  --- Pays Premium Upfront --->  [ Short Put (Seller/Writer) ]
                      <--- Grants Right to Sell ---
```

### Participant Positions

#### 1. Long Put (Put Buyer)

- **Market Outlook:** Bearish (expects underlying price to drop significantly).
    
      
    
- **Cash Flow:** Immediate cash outflow (pays total premium = $\text{Premium} \times \text{Lot Size}$).
    
      
    
- **Right vs. Obligation:** Holds the **right** to sell shares at strike price $K$.
    
      
    
- **Maximum Loss:** Strictly capped at the total premium paid.
    
      
    
- **Maximum Gain:** Substantial, capped when the asset price drops to zero: $(K - P) \times \text{Lot Size}$.
    
      
    

#### 2. Short Put (Put Seller / Writer)

- **Market Outlook:** Bullish or Neutral (expects price to stay above or at strike price $K$).
    
      
    
- **Cash Flow:** Immediate cash inflow (receives total premium).
    
      
    
- **Right vs. Obligation:** Holds the **obligation** to buy shares at $K$ if the buyer exercises.
    
      
    
- **Maximum Gain:** Strictly capped at the upfront premium received.
    
      
    
- **Maximum Loss:** Substantial, realized if price falls to zero: $(K - P) \times \text{Lot Size}$.
    
      
    

### Payoff Mathematics at Expiration

For a Put Option with strike price $K$ and premium $P$:

  

$$\text{Gross Payoff (Long Put)} = \max(K - S_T, 0)$$

$$\text{Net Profit/Loss (Long Put)} = \max(K - S_T, 0) - P$$

$$\text{Net Profit/Loss (Short Put)} = P - \max(K - S_T, 0)$$

$$\text{Break-Even Point (BEP)} = K - P$$

- **Put Premium ($P$):** ₹10 per share
    
      
    
- **Lot Size:** 1,000 shares
    
      
    
- **Total Premium Paid:** $1,000 \times ₹10 = ₹10,000$
    
      
    
- **Break-Even Point:** $₹250 - ₹10 = ₹240$

### Core Strategic Applications of Put Options

**1. The Protective Put (Portfolio Insurance)**

  

- **Mechanism:** Holding underlying long shares while simultaneously purchasing an equivalent number of Out-of-the-Money/At-the-Money Put options.
    
      
    
- **Objective:** Establishes a guaranteed price floor ($K$) below which the portfolio value cannot fall, while preserving 100% of upside capital appreciation (minus the put premium paid).
    
      
    

**2. The Cash-Secured Put (Target Acquisition Strategy)**

  

- **Mechanism:** Selling a put option at a desired strike price below current market price while setting aside cash to buy the stock if assigned.
    
      
    
- **Objective:** If the stock remains above $K$, the seller retains the premium as income. If the stock falls below $K$, the seller acquires the stock at an effective cost of $K - P$, which is lower than the original market price.
    
      
    

## 5. Deconstruction of Option Pricing: Intrinsic Value vs. Time Value

Every option premium quoted on an exchange is composed of two distinct components:

  

$$\text{Option Premium} = \text{Intrinsic Value (IV)} + \text{Time Value (TV)}$$

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                             OPTION PREMIUM                            |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
|       INTRINSIC VALUE (IV)        |          TIME VALUE (TV)          |
|  - Realizable value right now     |  - Value of remaining time        |
|  - Formula-driven: max(S-K,0)     |  - Reflects volatility & duration |
|  - Cannot be negative             |  - Decays to zero at expiration   |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
```

### Component Breakdown

#### 1. Intrinsic Value (IV)

The immediate, tangible economic value of the option if it were exercised at the current spot price. Intrinsic value can **never be negative**; if exercising yields a loss, IV is zero.

  

$$\text{Call Option IV} = \max(S - K, 0)$$

$$\text{Put Option IV} = \max(K - S, 0)$$

#### 2. Time Value (Extrinsic Value / TV)

The premium component reflecting the probability that the underlying asset's price will move favorably before expiration. It represents compensation to the option seller for assuming market uncertainty.

  

$$\text{Time Value} = \text{Total Option Premium} - \text{Intrinsic Value}$$

- **Time Decay ($\theta$):** As the option approaches its expiration date ($T \to 0$), the time value steadily declines, reaching exactly zero at expiration.
    
      
    
- **Volatility ($\sigma$):** Higher expected market volatility increases the time value for both calls and puts, because larger price swings increase the likelihood of the option finishing in profit.
    
      
    

### Option Moneyness Classifications

Moneyness describes the relationship between the strike price ($K$) of an option and the current spot price ($S$) of the underlying asset:

  

```
                  CALL OPTIONS                                PUT OPTIONS
  +-----------------------------------------+   +-----------------------------------------+
  | In-The-Money (ITM)     : Spot > Strike  |   | In-The-Money (ITM)     : Spot < Strike  |
  | At-The-Money (ATM)     : Spot = Strike  |   | At-The-Money (ATM)     : Spot = Strike  |
  | Out-Of-The-Money (OTM) : Spot < Strike  |   | Out-Of-The-Money (OTM) : Spot > Strike  |
  +-----------------------------------------+   +-----------------------------------------+
```

- **In-The-Money (ITM):** Holds positive intrinsic value ($\text{IV} > 0$). Premium consists of both IV and TV.
    
      
    
- **At-The-Money (ATM):** Strike price equals spot price ($S \approx K$). Intrinsic value is ₹0. Premium consists purely of maximum Time Value.
    
      
    
- **Out-of-the-Money (OTM):** Zero intrinsic value ($\text{IV} = 0$). Exercising would produce an immediate loss. Premium consists purely of Time Value.
    
      
    

### Strike Price vs. Premium Dynamics

The option chain exhibits mathematical relationships across strike prices for a common expiration date:

  

|**Option Type**|**Strike Price Movement**|**Premium Behavior**|**Economic Rationale**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Call Option**|Higher Strike ($\uparrow$)|**Lower Premium ($\downarrow$)**|Higher strike reduces the right to buy at an advantageous price; decreases probability of exercise.|
|**Call Option**|Lower Strike ($\downarrow$)|**Higher Premium ($\uparrow$)**|Lower strike gives the right to buy at a discounted price; builds in greater intrinsic value.|
|**Put Option**|Higher Strike ($\uparrow$)|**Higher Premium ($\uparrow$)**|Higher strike gives the right to sell at an elevated price; builds in greater intrinsic value.|
|**Put Option**|Lower Strike ($\downarrow$)|**Lower Premium ($\downarrow$)**|Lower strike requires asset to crash further to gain value; decreases probability of exercise.|

## 6. Secondary Market Trading vs. Physical Settlement

A widespread misconception among beginner investors is that buying an option requires holding it until expiration and physically taking delivery of the shares.

  

In modern exchange environments, market participants operate in two distinct ways:

  

### 1. Exercising the Contract (Maturity Settlement)

The option holder maintains the position until expiration ($T$). If the contract finishes In-The-Money ($\text{ITM}$), the exchange settles the trade by either:

  

- **Physical Settlement:** Delivering or taking delivery of actual underlying shares in exchange for cash value ($K \times \text{Lot Size}$).
    
      
    
- **Cash Settlement:** Directly crediting the net intrinsic profit ($\text{IV} \times \text{Lot Size}$) into the holder's brokerage account.
    
      
    

### 2. Squaring Off in the Secondary Market (Pre-Expiry Trading)

The majority of retail and institutional options volume is settled **before expiration**. Because exchange-traded options are liquid, standardized instruments, a long position can be closed at any time by executing an offsetting sale order at the current market premium.

  

$$\text{Trading P\&L} = (\text{Selling Premium} - \text{Purchase Premium}) \times \text{Lot Size}$$

#### Trading Example

1. **Entry:** Trader buys 1 lot (1,000 shares) of ₹270 Call Option on Zomato at ₹2.75 premium ($\text{Capital Outlay} = ₹2,750$).
    
      
    
2. **Market Move:** Within 5 trading days, Zomato spot price surges to ₹300.
    
      
    
3. **Repricing:** Due to deep intrinsic value expansion, the option premium rises from ₹2.75 to ₹35.00.
    
      
    
4. **Exit:** Trader sells the call option contract back to the secondary market at ₹35.00 without taking stock delivery.
    
      
    

$$\text{Net Profit} = (₹35.00 - ₹2.75) \times 1,000 = ₹32,250$$

$$\text{Return on Capital} = \frac{₹32,250}{₹2,750} \times 100 = 1,172.7\%$$

## 7. The Zero-Sum Framework of Derivatives

Options markets represent a closed, **zero-sum system**. For every rupee gained by an option buyer, an exact matching loss is incurred by the counterparty option seller, and vice versa (excluding exchange transaction costs and brokerage fees).

$$\sum \text{P\&L}_{\text{Buyer}} + \sum \text{P\&L}_{\text{Seller}} = 0$$

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                              ZERO-SUM PAYOFF DYNAMICS                             |
+--------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------+
| Long Position (Buyer)    | Short Position (Seller / Writer)                       |
+--------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------+
| Limited Loss (Premium)   | Limited Profit (Premium)                               |
| Unlimited/High Upside    | Unlimited/High Downside                                |
| Pays Time Value          | Earns Time Value (Benefits from Time Decay)            |
| Holds the Discretion     | Holds the Legal Obligation                             |
+--------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 4. Put Options (The Right to Sell)

A **Put Option** grants the contract buyer the legal right—without any obligation—to **sell** the underlying asset at the strike price ($K$) on or before the expiry date ($T$).

  

```
[ Long Put (Buyer) ]  --- Pays Premium Upfront --->  [ Short Put (Seller/Writer) ]
                      <--- Grants Right to Sell ---
```

### Participant Positions

#### 1. Long Put (Put Buyer)

- **Market Outlook:** Bearish (expects underlying price to drop significantly).
    
      
    
- **Cash Flow:** Immediate cash outflow (pays total premium = $\text{Premium} \times \text{Lot Size}$).
    
      
    
- **Right vs. Obligation:** Holds the **right** to sell shares at strike price $K$.
    
      
    
- **Maximum Loss:** Strictly capped at the total premium paid.
    
      
    
- **Maximum Gain:** Substantial, capped when the asset price drops to zero: $(K - P) \times \text{Lot Size}$.
    
      
    

#### 2. Short Put (Put Seller / Writer)

- **Market Outlook:** Bullish or Neutral (expects price to stay above or at strike price $K$).
    
      
    
- **Cash Flow:** Immediate cash inflow (receives total premium).
    
      
    
- **Right vs. Obligation:** Holds the **obligation** to buy shares at $K$ if the buyer exercises.
    
      
    
- **Maximum Gain:** Strictly capped at the upfront premium received.
    
      
    
- **Maximum Loss:** Substantial, realized if price falls to zero: $(K - P) \times \text{Lot Size}$.
    
      
    

### Payoff Mathematics at Expiration

For a Put Option with strike price $K$ and premium $P$:

  

$$\text{Gross Payoff (Long Put)} = \max(K - S_T, 0)$$

$$\text{Net Profit/Loss (Long Put)} = \max(K - S_T, 0) - P$$

$$\text{Net Profit/Loss (Short Put)} = P - \max(K - S_T, 0)$$

$$\text{Break-Even Point (BEP)} = K - P$$

### Core Strategic Applications of Put Options

**1. The Protective Put (Portfolio Insurance)**

  

- **Mechanism:** Holding underlying long shares while simultaneously purchasing an equivalent number of Out-of-the-Money/At-the-Money Put options.
    
      
    
- **Objective:** Establishes a guaranteed price floor ($K$) below which the portfolio value cannot fall, while preserving 100% of upside capital appreciation (minus the put premium paid).
    
      
    

**2. The Cash-Secured Put (Target Acquisition Strategy)**

  

- **Mechanism:** Selling a put option at a desired strike price below current market price while setting aside cash to buy the stock if assigned.
    
      
    
- **Objective:** If the stock remains above $K$, the seller retains the premium as income. If the stock falls below $K$, the seller acquires the stock at an effective cost of $K - P$, which is lower than the original market price.
    
      
    

## 5. Deconstruction of Option Pricing: Intrinsic Value vs. Time Value

Every option premium quoted on an exchange is composed of two distinct components:

  

$$\text{Option Premium} = \text{Intrinsic Value (IV)} + \text{Time Value (TV)}$$

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                             OPTION PREMIUM                            |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
|       INTRINSIC VALUE (IV)        |          TIME VALUE (TV)          |
|  - Realizable value right now     |  - Value of remaining time        |
|  - Formula-driven: max(S-K,0)     |  - Reflects volatility & duration |
|  - Cannot be negative             |  - Decays to zero at expiration   |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
```

### Component Breakdown

#### 1. Intrinsic Value (IV)

The immediate, tangible economic value of the option if it were exercised at the current spot price. Intrinsic value can **never be negative**; if exercising yields a loss, IV is zero.

  

$$\text{Call Option IV} = \max(S - K, 0)$$

$$\text{Put Option IV} = \max(K - S, 0)$$

#### 2. Time Value (Extrinsic Value / TV)

The premium component reflecting the probability that the underlying asset's price will move favorably before expiration. It represents compensation to the option seller for assuming market uncertainty.

  

$$\text{Time Value} = \text{Total Option Premium} - \text{Intrinsic Value}$$

- **Time Decay ($\theta$):** As the option approaches its expiration date ($T \to 0$), the time value steadily declines, reaching exactly zero at expiration.
    
      
    
- **Volatility ($\sigma$):** Higher expected market volatility increases the time value for both calls and puts, because larger price swings increase the likelihood of the option finishing in profit.
    
      
    

### Option Moneyness Classifications

Moneyness describes the relationship between the strike price ($K$) of an option and the current spot price ($S$) of the underlying asset:

  

```
                  CALL OPTIONS                                PUT OPTIONS
  +-----------------------------------------+   +-----------------------------------------+
  | In-The-Money (ITM)     : Spot > Strike  |   | In-The-Money (ITM)     : Spot < Strike  |
  | At-The-Money (ATM)     : Spot = Strike  |   | At-The-Money (ATM)     : Spot = Strike  |
  | Out-Of-The-Money (OTM) : Spot < Strike  |   | Out-Of-The-Money (OTM) : Spot > Strike  |
  +-----------------------------------------+   +-----------------------------------------+
```

- **In-The-Money (ITM):** Holds positive intrinsic value ($\text{IV} > 0$). Premium consists of both IV and TV.
    
      
    
- **At-The-Money (ATM):** Strike price equals spot price ($S \approx K$). Intrinsic value is ₹0. Premium consists purely of maximum Time Value.
    
      
    
- **Out-of-the-Money (OTM):** Zero intrinsic value ($\text{IV} = 0$). Exercising would produce an immediate loss. Premium consists purely of Time Value.
    
      
    

### Strike Price vs. Premium Dynamics

The option chain exhibits mathematical relationships across strike prices for a common expiration date:

  

|**Option Type**|**Strike Price Movement**|**Premium Behavior**|**Economic Rationale**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Call Option**|Higher Strike ($\uparrow$)|**Lower Premium ($\downarrow$)**|Higher strike reduces the right to buy at an advantageous price; decreases probability of exercise.|
|**Call Option**|Lower Strike ($\downarrow$)|**Higher Premium ($\uparrow$)**|Lower strike gives the right to buy at a discounted price; builds in greater intrinsic value.|
|**Put Option**|Higher Strike ($\uparrow$)|**Higher Premium ($\uparrow$)**|Higher strike gives the right to sell at an elevated price; builds in greater intrinsic value.|
|**Put Option**|Lower Strike ($\downarrow$)|**Lower Premium ($\downarrow$)**|Lower strike requires asset to crash further to gain value; decreases probability of exercise.|

## 6. Secondary Market Trading vs. Physical Settlement

A widespread misconception among beginner investors is that buying an option requires holding it until expiration and physically taking delivery of the shares.

  

In modern exchange environments, market participants operate in two distinct ways:

  

### 1. Exercising the Contract (Maturity Settlement)

The option holder maintains the position until expiration ($T$). If the contract finishes In-The-Money ($\text{ITM}$), the exchange settles the trade by either:

  

- **Physical Settlement:** Delivering or taking delivery of actual underlying shares in exchange for cash value ($K \times \text{Lot Size}$).
    
      
    
- **Cash Settlement:** Directly crediting the net intrinsic profit ($\text{IV} \times \text{Lot Size}$) into the holder's brokerage account.
    
      
    

### 2. Squaring Off in the Secondary Market (Pre-Expiry Trading)

The majority of retail and institutional options volume is settled **before expiration**. Because exchange-traded options are liquid, standardized instruments, a long position can be closed at any time by executing an offsetting sale order at the current market premium.

  

$$\text{Trading P\&L} = (\text{Selling Premium} - \text{Purchase Premium}) \times \text{Lot Size}$$

#### Trading Example

1. **Entry:** Trader buys 1 lot (1,000 shares) of ₹270 Call Option on Zomato at ₹2.75 premium ($\text{Capital Outlay} = ₹2,750$).
    
      
    
2. **Market Move:** Within 5 trading days, Zomato spot price surges to ₹300.
    
      
    
3. **Repricing:** Due to deep intrinsic value expansion, the option premium rises from ₹2.75 to ₹35.00.
    
      
    
4. **Exit:** Trader sells the call option contract back to the secondary market at ₹35.00 without taking stock delivery.
    
      
    

$$\text{Net Profit} = (₹35.00 - ₹2.75) \times 1,000 = ₹32,250$$

$$\text{Return on Capital} = \frac{₹32,250}{₹2,750} \times 100 = 1,172.7\%$$

## 7. The Zero-Sum Framework of Derivatives

Options markets represent a closed, **zero-sum system**. For every rupee gained by an option buyer, an exact matching loss is incurred by the counterparty option seller, and vice versa (excluding exchange transaction costs and brokerage fees).

$$\sum \text{P\&L}_{\text{Buyer}} + \sum \text{P\&L}_{\text{Seller}} = 0$$

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                              ZERO-SUM PAYOFF DYNAMICS                             |
+--------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------+
| Long Position (Buyer)    | Short Position (Seller / Writer)                       |
+--------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------+
| Limited Loss (Premium)   | Limited Profit (Premium)                               |
| Unlimited/High Upside    | Unlimited/High Downside                                |
| Pays Time Value          | Earns Time Value (Benefits from Time Decay)            |
| Holds the Discretion     | Holds the Legal Obligation                             |
+--------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------+
```
