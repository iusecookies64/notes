
## Foundations of Natural Language Processing (NLP)

### 1. First-Principles Definition & Core Objective

Human language is a symbolic, discrete, and highly contextual communication protocol that evolved for human-to-human interaction, whereas computers operate deterministically on structured numerical data.

* **Definition:** NLP is an interdisciplinary field at the intersection of computer science, artificial intelligence, and linguistics. It enables computational systems to read, decipher, understand, and generate human language.

* **Core Challenge:** Human language is inherently unstructured, highly ambiguous, and deeply dependent on context. NLP functions as the computational translation layer between human communication and machine processing.

* **High-Level Goals:**
	* **Ingestion & Interpretation:** Parsing raw text to extract syntactic and semantic structures.
	
	* **Information Extraction:** Mining structured data and entities from unstructured corpora.
	
	* **Response Synthesis:** Generating coherent, contextually accurate natural language outputs.

#### Natural Language Understanding (NLU)

* **Objective:** Decodes the underlying meaning, structure, and intent behind text inputs.

* **Core Tasks:**
	* *Named Entity Recognition (NER):* Identifying and tagging proper nouns, dates, locations, and organizations.
	
	* *Sentiment Analysis:* Classifying subjective polarity (positive, negative, neutral).
	
	* *Text Classification:* Categorizing documents or intent detection (e.g., spam vs. ham filtering).

#### Natural Language Generation (NLG)

* **Objective:** Transforms structured information or representations into coherent, human-readable text.

* **Core Tasks:**
	* *Machine Translation:* Mapping semantics across different linguistic systems.
	
	* *Text Summarization:* Compressing verbose text into salient key points.
	
	* *Conversational Systems:* Generating context-aware responses in chatbots and large language models (LLMs).

> **System Integration:** Real-world NLP architectures (e.g., conversational AI, voice assistants) pipeline NLU to comprehend user prompts and NLG to synthesize relevant responses.

### 3. Linguistic Foundations: Morphology

* **Definition:** The branch of linguistics dedicated to studying the internal structure, formation, and relationships of words.

* **Building Blocks (Morphemes):** Words are constructed from minimal units of meaning (e.g., *Root* "play" + *Suffix* "-ful" = "playful").

* **Significance in NLP Pipelines:**
	* **Vocabulary Reduction:** Prevents the vocabulary size from exploding by mapping inflected/derived word forms (e.g., *running*, *runs*, *ran*) back to a common base.
	
	* **Generalization:** Allows models to infer the meaning of unseen compound words based on known constituent subwords.
	
	* **Tokenization Strategies:** Directly motivates modern subword tokenizers (e.g., Byte-Pair Encoding, WordPiece) that break rare words into recognizable morphological units.

### 4. Semantic Ambiguities & Linguistic Hurdles

Machines fail when mapping words strictly one-to-one with concepts due to intrinsic linguistic ambiguity.

* **Linguistic Ambiguity:** Occurs when a single string of text yields multiple valid syntactic or semantic interpretations.
	
	* *Example:* *"I saw her duck"* (Interpretable as observing an action of ducking or perceiving a physical waterfowl).

* **Polysemy:** A single word possessing multiple context-dependent meanings.

	* *Examples:*
		* *Bank:* Financial institution vs. Riverbank.
		
		* *Run:* Physical exercise vs. Operating an algorithm vs. Managing an organization.
		
		* *Paper:* Physical medium vs. Academic research publication.
	
	* *Implication:* Static dictionary lookups fail; word representations must be dynamically conditioned on surrounding context.

* **Synonymy:** Distinct words conveying equivalent or closely overlapping semantic meaning (e.g., *buy* $\leftrightarrow$ *purchase*; *big* $\leftrightarrow$ *large*).
	* *Implication:* Causes vocabulary sparsity and makes naive exact-string matching ineffective.

* **Resolution via Vector Embeddings:** Modern NLP maps words into dense, continuous vector spaces (e.g., Word2Vec, Transformer embeddings) where semantic similarity corresponds to geometric proximity (e.g., cosine distance).
