
## Text Corpora, Corpus Linguistics & Statistical Foundations

### 1. First Principles: The Corpus-Driven Statistical Paradigm

Modern Natural Language Processing operates on a statistical paradigm rather than deterministic, rule-based linguistics. Machines process text through numerical representations and probability distributions learned directly from empirical language samples.

```

  │ article)     │           │ language use │            │ task super-  │
  │              │           │              │            │ vision       │
  └──────────────┘           └──────────────┘            └──────────────┘

```

* **The Primary Role of Corpora:** Corpora provide underlying natural language distributions, while datasets provide targeted task supervision.

* **Statistical Dependencies in NLP Tasks:**
	* *TF-IDF:* Derived from lexical term and document frequencies.
	
	* *Word Embeddings:* Derived from spatial token co-occurrence matrices.
	
	* *Topic Modeling:* Derived from document-word Dirichlet distributions.
	
	* *Language Modeling:* Derived from sequential $n$-gram and autoregressive conditional token probabilities.

* **The GIGO Principle:** Machine learning models reflect the statistical properties of their inputs (*Garbage In, Garbage Out*). Any change, skew, or deficiency in the training corpus alters model behavior.

### 3. Data Bias & Sociotechnical Representation

Models do not spontaneously invent social biases; they systematically internalize, optimize, and amplify statistical regularities present in the underlying corpus.

* **Dimensions of Corpus Bias:**
	* **Source Bias:** Text reflects the editorial or demographic perspective of its medium (e.g., formal news outlets vs. conversational social media feeds).
	
	* **Representation Bias:** Unequal sampling that underrepresents minority viewpoints, regional dialects, or specific demographic cohorts.
	
	* **Historical Bias:** Retaining archaic cultural stereotypes, obsolete terminology, and historical socioeconomic inequalities present in legacy texts.
	
	* **Annotation Bias:** Subjective judgment, cultural presuppositions, and label noise introduced by human annotators during dataset creation.

* **Downstream Manifestations:**
	* *Stereotypical Geometry in Embeddings:* Word vectors mirroring historical gender divisions (e.g., $\vec{v}_{\text{doctor}} - \vec{v}_{\text{man}} + \vec{v}_{\text{woman}} \approx \vec{v}_{\text{nurse}}$).
	
	* *Performance Disparities:* Higher error rates when evaluating models on underrepresented dialects or specialized demographic vernaculars.

### 4. Quantitative Corpus Linguistics & Metrics

Corpus analysis prevents treating language data as an uninspected black box by profiling vocabulary distributions and lexical complexity.

* **Tokens ($N$) vs. Types ($V$):**
	* *Token Count ($N$):* The total running count of all individual word instances in a corpus.
	
	* *Vocabulary Size ($\vert{}V\vert{}$):* The count of unique word forms (types) in the corpus lexicon.

* **Type-Token Ratio (TTR):**
$$\text{TTR} = \frac{\vert{}V\vert{}}{N}$$
	* Quantifies lexical diversity and vocabulary richness.
	* *Interpretation:* Higher TTR values indicate greater descriptive variance and varied vocabulary (common in creative literature); lower TTR values reflect repetitive, standardized vocabulary structures.

* **Zipfian Distribution & Heavy Tails:**
	* Natural language corpora exhibit power-law word frequency distributions.
	
	* A tiny fraction of function/stop words (*the*, *of*, *and*, *is*) dominates total word counts without providing topical discrimination.
	
	* Filtering stop words exposes domain-specific "content signatures" (e.g., nouns and action verbs), uncovering the corpus's thematic focus.

### 5. Empirical Case Study: Gutenberg vs. Brown Corpus

```python
import nltk
from nltk.corpus import brown, gutenberg
from collections import Counter

# Inspecting lexical statistics
gutenberg_words = [w.lower() for w in gutenberg.words('carroll-alice.txt') if w.isalpha()]
brown_news_words = [w.lower() for w in brown.words(categories='news') if w.isalpha()]
brown_fiction_words = [w.lower() for w in brown.words(categories='fiction') if w.isalpha()]

```

* **Project Gutenberg (*Alice in Wonderland*):**
	* Total Tokens ($N$): ~27,333 cleaned alphabetic words.
	
	* Unique Vocabulary ($\vert{}V\vert{}$): ~2,569 unique words.
	
	* Lexical Richness: $\text{TTR} \approx 0.094$, showing a concentrated vocabulary dominated by narrative character dialogue.

* **The Brown Corpus (Cross-Genre Distribution):**
	* **Scale:** Contains ~1.16M words across 15 distinct genres (e.g., *News*, *Fiction*, *Government*, *Learned*).
	
	* **News Category ($N \approx 100\text{k}$):** Exhibits formal, institutional terminology (*said*, *investigation*, *jury*, *state*). Language is standardized and repetitive, yielding a lower comparative TTR.
	
	* **Fiction Category ($N \approx 68\text{k}$):** Exhibits high narrative and descriptive variation (*would*, *could*, *man*, *thought*), demonstrating higher lexical diversity per token count.

* **Custom Stop Word Engineering:** High-frequency, domain-dominant narrative words (e.g., adding *"said"* to custom stop word lists) must often be dynamically pruned to prevent statistical masking in downstream topic modeling and categorization.
