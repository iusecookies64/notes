## Computer Networks Basics

#### **Definition**
A computer network is a set of **Nodes** connected by **Communication Links**.
#### **Node In CN**
A node in computer network is a device that is capable to sending/receiving data.

Example - 
1. Computer
2. Mobile
3. Tablet
4. Printer
5. Camera
6. Switches
7. Bridges
8. Router, etc.
#### **Communication Link In CN**
A communication link is the medium through which data travels from one node to another. The communication link could be wired or wireless.

##### Ethernet Cable

**Ethernet Cable** which has twisted pair wires in it with RJ45 port at its ends, this is shown in the image below.

![[Pasted image 20250510113446.png]]

Ethernet cable transfers data via voltage (high voltage for 1 bit and low voltage for 0 bit), the sender applies a voltage which almost instantly (almost at speed of light) the receiver captures and converts into respective bit. How many times per-second this voltage changes defines the speed at which data is transferring. 

For e.g. 100 Mbps = 100 million bits per second, which means every `1/100,000,000` seconds a new voltage is placed on the wire.

Since at a time only one voltage can be applied on the wire we say Ethernet wires to be **Baseband** signalling medium i.e. at a time only one signal is transferred at a time using the entire bandwidth of the wire.

**Why Twist the Wires?**
This is to reduce cross talk between the wires. Cross talk is a type of electrical interference where signal transmitted on one wire or circuit interferes with signal on adjacent wire or circuit. 

Wires carrying current emit EM waves and these waves from two long parallel wires carrying can interfere with the signals in other wire there by corrupting the data increasing error rates and reducing performance.

**Twisting** the cables reduces this interference because the wires alternate positions and hence their net interference cancels out (This is where i stopped and didn't go in more depth 😄).

Another way to reduce cross talk is by shielding the cables with foil or other materials. These wires are more expensive and mostly used in large companies and government agencies who want to protect their data from being stolen using this radiation coming of the wires carrying the data.

The more tight the twists are, the better speeds we get. The early ethernet cables (Cat3) had tight twists and hence could only support speeds upto 10 Mbps. The newer (Cat5/Cat5e/Cat6) wires have tighter twists.

> [!Note]
> Cat3, Cat5, Cat5e etc are simply short form for Category 3/5/5e (e=Enhanced) etc. These different categories are defined based on different performance standards like data transfer rate, max bandwidth, cable quality, max cable length, signal-to-noise and crosstalk.

There are many different types of Ethernet cables each varying in terms of the maximum speed it supports, the medium and topology. The naming convention for these wires defined by IEEE (Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers) 802.11 is of the following format.

`<speed><base><medium/topology>`

Below are few examples.
1. 10Base-T: 10 Mbps Baseband Twisted pair (1 pair). Used in Cat3 or Cat5 cables.
2. 100Base-Tx: 100 Mbps Baseband Twisted pair (2 pair). Used in Cat5 or better.
3. 1000Base-T: 1 Gbps Baseband Twisted pair (4 pairs). Used in Cat5e, Cat6.
4. 1000Base-Sx: 1 Gbps Baseband Short wavelength laser over fibre.
5. 10GBase-T: 10 Gbps Baseband twister pair used in Cat6a or better.

##### Coaxial Cable

Coaxial cable is a made up of multiple layers at the centre of which we have a copper wire. This is shown in the below diagram.

The signal in this wire doesn't travel as electric signal, rather these are em waves travelling between the centre conductor and the metal shield. The Electric Field goes radially outwards from the centre and the magnetic field goes in circle inside the shield.
###### 3. **Modulation: How Bits Ride on Waves**

Modulation is what **encodes digital data** onto analog waves. Coax systems often use:
- **Amplitude Modulation (AM)** — varies signal strength
- **Frequency Modulation (FM)** — varies frequency slightly
- **Quadrature Amplitude Modulation (QAM)** — varies amplitude + phase (used in cable internet)
- **Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM)** — uses many subcarriers, each modulated separately (used in DOCSIS 3.1+)

So your 0s and 1s are not sent as voltages like in Ethernet — they **modify a continuous analog waveform** at a specific frequency.

At a time a coaxial cable can have multiple different waves going through it, hence it can be used as a broadband. Different types of data signals are converted to different range of frequency and the receiver demodulates them back into data. This is shown in the table below.

| Frequency Range | Signal Type                |
| --------------- | -------------------------- |
| 88–108 MHz      | FM radio                   |
| 174–216 MHz     | VHF TV (e.g. Channel 7–13) |
| 500–800 MHz     | Digital TV channels        |
| 5–85 MHz        | Upstream cable internet    |
| 108–1000+ MHz   | Downstream internet data   |

Coaxial cables are legacy hardware and not used much now.
##### Fibre Optic Cable

Fibre optic cable uses light instead of electricity to transfer data. The fibre optic cable is shown below.

![[Pasted image 20250510140405.png]]

The light is kept inside the cable using something known as total internal reflection.

A pulse of light means 1 bit and no pulse means 0 bit. Usually these photons are sent from labels or LED's.

Fibre optic cable supports huge bandwidths that can carry many data streams at once. There are two ways in which this is done.

**Wavelength-Division Multiplexing (WDM)**
Each data stream is carried out in a different wavelength light, these different wavelengths are combined into a single beam and sent down the fibre.

**Different Modes of Propagation (in MMF)**
In Multimode Fibre (MMF) the core is made wider which allows different paths to exist within the wire that the light can take. Each path can be used to carry separate streams (to a limited extent).

In practice, a single-mode fibre (SMF) + WDM is used for long distance high capacity links.

Fibre optic cable supports speed upto 100+ Gbps.

## Types Of Data Communication

There are three types of data communication in computer network, they are
1. Simplex
2. Half Duplex
3. Full Duplex
#### Simplex Communication

In Simplex Communication the data transfer is unidirectional i.e. one can either send or receive data not do both. For example consider radio broadcast, in this case the radio station is the transmitter and then there are many receivers.

#### Half Duplex Communication

In Half Duplex Communication, the data transfer is bidirectional i.e. both connected devices can send or receive data. But the catch here is that they can't do this at the same time i.e. at a time they can either be sending data or receiving it.

A good example of this is Walkie Talkie devices, in which you always receive the data from others and when you want to speak you press a button and start speaking. When you are speaking you won't receive any data from other walkie talkies.

#### Full Duplex Communication

In Full Duplex Communication we can send and receive the data at the same time. A good example of this is telephones in which we can talk and while talking also receive sound of person on the other side of the call.

## Peer To Peer Vs Client Server Network
#### Peer-to-Peer (P2P) Network

- Every device (called a **peer**) acts as both a **client and a server**.
- Peers share resources (files, processing power, etc.) **directly** with each other without a central server.
- Common in **file sharing systems**, **cryptocurrencies**, and **torrenting**.

**Example**: Bitcoin network, BitTorrent
#### Client-Server Network

- A **central server** provides services or resources to **multiple client** devices.
- Clients send requests to the server, and the server responds.
- Common in **web applications**, **email systems**, and **corporate networks**.

**Example**: A website server that delivers pages to users' browsers.

| Feature              | Peer-to-Peer (P2P)                               | Client-Server                                       |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Architecture**     | Decentralized                                    | Centralized                                         |
| **Server Role**      | No dedicated server                              | Central server controls services/data               |
| **Resource Sharing** | Shared among peers                               | Provided by the server                              |
| **Scalability**      | Can be more scalable                             | May become bottlenecked at the server               |
| **Reliability**      | More fault-tolerant (no single point of failure) | Less fault-tolerant (if server fails, system fails) |
| **Setup Complexity** | Simpler setup                                    | More complex setup                                  |
| **Security**         | Typically lower, since no central control        | Easier to secure centrally                          |
| **Examples**         | Bitcoin, BitTorrent, Skype (P2P version)         | Gmail, Facebook, Online Banking                     |

## Topologies of Computer Network

In general we use 5 types of topologies in computer network,
1. Mesh Topology
2. Star Topology
3. Bus Topology
4. Ring Topology
5. Hybrid Topology
#### Mesh Topology

In mesh topology all the nodes are connected with each other. Hence if there are n nodes in the mesh topology, then the number of links we need can by calculated using the formula:

$^nC_2 = n(n-1) / 2$.

Number of ports needed = 2 * Number of Links = n(n-1) 

![[Pasted image 20250511080116.png]]

In mesh topology, the reliability is very high i.e. if a node goes down, the network still keeps on working. Also the security is very high since there is no one to intercept the connection.

The cost of setting up a mesh topology is very high and complicated.

It provides point to point connection i.e. two nodes can directly talk to each other without any other node in the way.
#### Star Topology

In star topology, all nodes are connected to a single central device, this device could be a network hub, switch, router or an access point (for wireless star topology).

The number of cables = $n$ and the number of ports = $2*n$

![[Pasted image 20250511083539.png]]

Reliability is very less, because if the central device fails then the network gets down.

Cost is low, majority of the cost is of the central hub.

Security is very less, since all of the data is passing through the hub, if hub gets compromised, so does every device in the network.

This also provides point to point connection as a node can directly talk to another node using the hub without any other node coming in.
#### Bus Topology

In bus topology, all of the nodes are connected to a single central cable often called the backbone of the network. The nodes connect to the bus using T-connector (Tap) which taps into the central cable to get the data being transmitted. At the end of the connection there are terminators present which prevent the signal from bouncing and creating a new unwanted signal.

![[Pasted image 20250511083644.png]]

The number of cables required = $n+1$

The number of ports = $n$

Reliability is very less because of the central cable goes down, then the network goes down along with it.

Cost of setup is very less and security is also very less.

This is a multi point connection i.e. two nodes cannot talk in isolation, the data sent by a node is received on every other node.
#### Ring Topology

In ring topology, all nodes are connected in a ring manner or circular manner. If a cable or a node fails, then the entire network goes down. So we can say that all cables are backbone and on top of that there is dependency on the nodes as well so if a node goes down, so does the network.

![[Pasted image 20250511084605.png]]

The number of cables = $n$

The number of ports = $2 * n$

The cost is very less, the security is also very less.

This is also a multi point connection.
#### Hybrid Topology

A **hybrid topology** is a network structure that combines two or more different types of basic topologies—like star, bus, ring, or mesh—into one larger network. This is commonly used in large organisation where different departments may use different topologies, and all are interconnected.

![[Pasted image 20250511085444.png]]

**Examples of Hybrid Topology**
- **Star-Bus Hybrid**: Several star-configured networks are connected via a common bus line.
- **Star-Ring Hybrid**: Multiple star networks connected in a ring structure.
- **Tree Topology**: A special kind of hybrid that connects star-configured nodes in a hierarchical manner.

**Advantages of Hybrid Topology**
1. **Scalability:** Easy to add new nodes or networks without disrupting the existing system.
2. **Flexibility:** You can design the network to meet the needs of each specific department or application.
3. **Improved Performance:** Traffic can be isolated and optimised within each topology segment.
4. **Fault Isolation:**  Failure in one part of the network (e.g., a ring segment) doesn’t necessarily affect the rest.
5. **Customisation:** Suitable for complex organisational needs by combining the strengths of various topologies.

**Disadvantages of Hybrid Topology**
1. **Complex Design and Management:**  Requires careful planning and skilled network administrators.
2. **High Cost:** Involves more hardware (switches, routers) and maintenance compared to simpler topologies.
3. **Troubleshooting Difficulties:**  Problems can be harder to diagnose due to the combination of multiple topologies.
4. **Cabling Requirements:** May require more cabling and infrastructure, depending on the types of topologies used.
