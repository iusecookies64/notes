# Computer Networks Basics

#### **Definition**
A computer network is a set of **Nodes** connected by **Communication Links**.
#### **Node In CN**
A node in computer network is a device that is capable to sending/receiving data.

Example - 
1. Computer
2. Mobile
3. Tablet
4. Printer
5. Camera
6. Switches
7. Bridges
8. Router, etc.
#### **Communication Link In CN**
A communication link is the medium through which data travels from one node to another. The communication link could be wired or wireless.

The most common wired communication links are shown below.
##### Ethernet Cable

**Ethernet Cable** which has twisted pair wires in it with RJ45 port at its ends, this is shown in the image below.

![[Pasted image 20250510113446.png]]

Ethernet cable transfers data via voltage (high voltage for 1 bit and low voltage for 0 bit), the sender applies a voltage which almost instantly (almost at speed of light) the receiver captures and converts into respective bit. How many times per-second this voltage changes defines the speed at which data is transferring. 

For e.g. 100 Mbps = 100 million bits per second, which means every `1/100,000,000` seconds a new voltage is placed on the wire.

Since at a time only one voltage can be applied on the wire we say Ethernet wires to be **Baseband** signalling medium i.e. at a time only one signal is transferred at a time using the entire bandwidth of the wire.

**Why Twist the Wires?**
This is to reduce cross talk between the wires. Cross talk is a type of electrical interference where signal transmitted on one wire or circuit interferes with signal on adjacent wire or circuit. 

Wires carrying current emit EM waves and these waves from two long parallel wires carrying can interfere with the signals in other wire there by corrupting the data increasing error rates and reducing performance.

**Twisting** the cables reduces this interference because the wires alternate positions and hence their net interference cancels out (This is where i stopped and didn't go in more depth 😄).

Another way to reduce cross talk is by shielding the cables with foil or other materials. These wires are more expensive and mostly used in large companies and government agencies who want to protect their data from being stolen using this radiation coming of the wires carrying the data.

The more tight the twists are, the better speeds we get. The early ethernet cables (Cat3) had tight twists and hence could only support speeds upto 10 Mbps. The newer (Cat5/Cat5e/Cat6) wires have tighter twists.

> [!Note]
> Cat3, Cat5, Cat5e etc are simply short form for Category 3/5/5e (e=Enhanced) etc. These different categories are defined based on different performance standards like data transfer rate, max bandwidth, cable quality, max cable length, signal-to-noise and crosstalk. 
> 
> Below is the table showing this.
> 
> |Category|Max Speed|Max Bandwidth|Max Distance|Use Case / Notes|
|---|---|---|---|---|

> 
> Cat5e is the most common type of cable we find in homes and small offices.

There are many different types of Ethernet cables each varying in terms of the maximum speed it supports, the medium and topology. The naming convention for these wires defined by IEEE (Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers) 802.11 is of the following format.

`<speed><base><medium/topology>`

Below are few examples.
1. 10Base-T: 10 Mbps Baseband Twisted pair (1 pair). Used in Cat3 or Cat5 cables.
2. 100Base-Tx: 100 Mbps Baseband Twisted pair (2 pair). Used in Cat5 or better.
3. 1000Base-T: 1 Gbps Baseband Twisted pair (4 pairs). Used in Cat5e, Cat6.
4. 1000Base-Sx: 1 Gbps Baseband Short wavelength laser over fibre.
5. 10GBase-T: 10 Gbps Baseband twister pair used in Cat6a or better.

The signal in this wire doesn't travel as electric signal, rather these are em waves travelling between the centre conductor and the metal shield. The Electric Field goes radially outwards from the centre and the magnetic field goes in circle inside the shield.
###### 3. **Modulation: How Bits Ride on Waves**

Modulation is what **encodes digital data** onto analog waves. Coax systems often use:
- **Amplitude Modulation (AM)** — varies signal strength
- **Frequency Modulation (FM)** — varies frequency slightly
- **Quadrature Amplitude Modulation (QAM)** — varies amplitude + phase (used in cable internet)
- **Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM)** — uses many subcarriers, each modulated separately (used in DOCSIS 3.1+)

So your 0s and 1s are not sent as voltages like in Ethernet — they **modify a continuous analog waveform** at a specific frequency.

At a time a coaxial cable can have multiple different waves going through it, hence it can be used as a broadband. Different types of data signals are converted to different range of frequency and the receiver demodulates them back into data. This is shown in the table below.

| Frequency Range | Signal Type                |
| --------------- | -------------------------- |
| 88–108 MHz      | FM radio                   |
| 174–216 MHz     | VHF TV (e.g. Channel 7–13) |
| 500–800 MHz     | Digital TV channels        |
| 5–85 MHz        | Upstream cable internet    |
| 108–1000+ MHz   | Downstream internet data   |

Coaxial cables are legacy hardware and not used much now.
##### Fibre Optic Cable

Fibre optic cable uses light instead of electricity to transfer data. The fibre optic cable is shown below.

![[Pasted image 20250510140405.png]]

The light is kept inside the cable using something known as total internal reflection.

A pulse of light means 1 bit and no pulse means 0 bit. Usually these photons are sent from labels or LED's.

Fibre optic cable supports huge bandwidths that can carry many data streams at once. There are two ways in which this is done.

**Wavelength-Division Multiplexing (WDM)**
Each data stream is carried out in a different wavelength light, these different wavelengths are combined into a single beam and sent down the fibre.

**Different Modes of Propagation (in MMF)**
In Multimode Fibre (MMF) the core is made wider which allows different paths to exist within the wire that the light can take. Each path can be used to carry separate streams (to a limited extent).

In practice, a single-mode fibre (SMF) + WDM is used for long distance high capacity links.

Fibre optic cable supports speed upto 100+ Gbps.

#### Wireless Communication Links

1. Infrared: used for short range (1-10m). Example TV remote
2. Radio: used for short range (1-100m). Example Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, Zigbee
3. Microwave: used for medium range (1-10km). Example Cellular systems
4. Satellite: used for long range (above 100km). Example dish tv, gps
