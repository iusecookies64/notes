
## Soul of Statistics

### Abraham Wald & Bullet Holes

During world war II, too many british bombers were getting shot down by the Nazi's, and the british army were having a hard time to decide where should be apply the extra armor. Now we would want to put the extra armor everywhere, but that will make the plane too heavy which, so this we cannot do.

They called in Abraham Wald to help them decide where should they put the extra armor. Now initially the army were thinking to apply armor where the returning planes received the most damage. But Wald suggest the opposite, he said to apply it to the parts that received the least/no hits, that is because the sample is heavily skewed by the planes that got hit and still survived.

Interestingly none of these planes have got damaged on the nose, which makes sense otherwise they would not have returned. Hence nose is an area where we must add extra armor, and same for other parts where the armor was applied.

![[Pasted image 20260817090750.png]]

This is a classic case of conditioning, we are looking at planes that survived despited being hit and we are trying to guess the bullet holes distribution on the planes that did not survive.

> [!Quote]
> Conditioning is the Soul of Statistics - **Joseph K. Blitzstein**

### Regression Towards the Mean

A lot of natural phenomenon tend to follow this rule which is extreme cases are followed by cases that tend towards the mean case.

**Example:** A tall father will most likely have son which is not as tall. Not to confuse, his son will still be tall, but probably not as tall as his father. Hence his height moved towards the mean.
![[regression_towards_the_mean.png|655]]
If this was not the case then we would see within few generations, we would have a population that comprises of dwarfs and giants. The above image shows this perfectly, where if every person has 3 children and each have equal probability of being taller, same height or shorter.

>[!Quote]
>We tend to reward an extremely good performance and punish a bad performance. But, since there is regression towards mean, the person we praised would likely perform worse next time, while the one we punished will likely perform better. It is hence part of human conditioning that we are pusnished for rewarding others and rewarded for punishing them. - **Daniel Kahneman Quoted**

## 1.2 Sample Space and Pebble World

One of the biggest breakthroughs in the study of probability was the use of sets to work with events. Set operations are perfect to express events and combination of events using unions, intersection & complements.

**Sample Space:** It is the set of all possible outcomes of an experement. It is denoted by S.

**Event:** It is a subset of the sample space. We say an event occurred if the outcome is in the subset.

**Venn Diagrams:** these diagrams help us in visualizing the sample space, events and set operations on events.
![[01. Probability and Counting-1786939573014.webp]]
The outer box is used to represent the sample space and the region inside the box are events.

A sample space can have finite, countably infinite, or uncountably infinite number of outcomes. When the number of outcomes are finite, then we can use the **Pebble World** version of the Venn Diagrams where each outcome is shown by a pebble. This is neatly shown in the diagram below.
